What Are Loose Gems?

These are individual gems added directly to your inventory while mining by the Stone Golem pet or the matching six-piece set. Each successful bonus-gem roll awards one gem; there is no bag to open.

02 / The Essentials

How Rare Gathering Work

01

Better Resources, Better Rates

The formula uses the resource’s required level, not your character’s level. Higher-tier trees, ore veins, and fish give more finds per hour. At the highest resource tier, the level factor reaches ×1.

02

Speed Balances Itself Out

Faster gathering gives more rolls, but each roll has a proportionally smaller chance. Tools, speed potions, and weather speed bonuses therefore do not normally raise the expected treasure rate per hour.

03

No Cooldown between Finds

Active play rolls independently for each completed action, awarding at most one container per action. Two consecutive actions can both succeed. “One nest every 90 seconds” is an average, not a scheduled drop.

04

Offline Uses Expected Totals

Offline and background gathering calculate the expected count, give the whole-number portion, then roll for one more using the fraction. An expectation of 12.7 gives 12 finds, plus a 70% chance of a 13th.

What actually increases finds? The resource tier, a matching Elder Treant / Stone Golem / Kraken pet, and matching treasure-find armor. General luck, VIP luck, combat drop bonuses, and pet whistles are not used by this formula. Armor set perks do not add another treasure-find multiplier.

What If Higher-Level Resources Are Added?

The game takes Lmax from the last resource in the skill’s list. If an update appends a higher-level resource and leaves this formula unchanged, existing resources produce fewer rare finds per hour because their L stays the same while Lmax rises. For example, raising woodcutting’s maximum resource level from 122 to 150 would reduce unboosted Aeonwood from 40 to about 35.15 nests/hour. The new level-150 resource would receive 40/hour. This guide uses the bundled 4.1.3.8 resource list.

Armor: Add Pieces, Then Multiply

For each equipped piece: bonus = base + (rarity multiplier − 1) × scaling. Add the pieces to obtain G. Pet and armor multipliers multiply each other; the armor pieces themselves add together.

Set Bonuses Are Included, without Double-Counting Speed

Three matching armor/tool pieces grant +5% gathering speed. At six pieces (five armor pieces and a matching boss tool), that becomes +15%, plus half of the boss pet’s extra-resource perk: a 5% triple-log chance, up to 6 pearls/hour, or up to 10 loose gems/hour. Pet and set perks add together. Pearl/gem rates also use the resource level factor.

Neither threshold grants treasure_find. The set therefore contributes ×1 to the nest/bag/chest formula. Its speed bonus is added to the gear gathering-speed bonus; faster actions then have proportionally smaller rare-drop chances. For example, adding a Treant Axe to the five-piece Elderwood example still gives 92 nests/hour, while unlocking +15% set speed and raising the equipped pet’s triple-log chance from 10% to 15%.

Per-Action Chance, Caps, and Timing Details

Active-play chance = min(1, hourly rate × adjusted action seconds ÷ 3,600). A successful roll grants one container. The pet doubles the chance rather than granting two containers from one success.

The hourly formula describes productive gathering time. UI tick timing and incomplete actions can slightly lower results per wall-clock hour. If an unusually long action would give a probability above 100%, active play is capped at one find per action; the optional action-time field shows that cap. Offline/background awards use the uncapped expected total and stochastic rounding instead.

Opening a container uses separate loot tables. This calculator estimates how many containers you find, not their contents, pet bonus pearls/gems, or skill-boss rewards.
